I'm excited to share that our paper **"S-SONDO: Self-Supervised Knowledge Distillation for General Audio Foundation Models"** has been accepted at **ICASSP 2026**!

This is joint work with Aurian Quelennec, Pierre Chouteau, Geoffroy Peeters, and Slim Essid at [LTCI, Telecom Paris, Institut Polytechnique de Paris](https://www.telecom-paris.fr/).

## The Problem

Audio foundation models like MATPAC++ and M2D achieve impressive performance across music and environmental sound tasks, but they are **too large** for deployment on edge devices or latency-sensitive applications. MATPAC++ alone has hundreds of millions of parameters. How do we get a tiny model that retains most of that knowledge?

## Our Approach

S-SONDO is the **first framework for self-supervised knowledge distillation of general audio foundation models**. The key idea is simple but effective:

1. **Extract teacher embeddings** from a pretrained audio foundation model on AudioSet (~2M samples)
2. **Cluster the embeddings** using MiniBatchKMeans to discover structure in the teacher's latent space
3. **Train a lightweight student** to map its embeddings to the teacher's space using cosine similarity loss, with **Balanced Data Sampling (BDS)** across clusters

What makes S-SONDO unique:
- **No logits or layer-level alignment needed** — only output embeddings
- The teacher is never run during training (embeddings are cached)
- BDS ensures the student sees a balanced view of the teacher's knowledge

The highlights:
- **61x model compression** with **96.4% performance retention**
- DyMN (6.7M params) achieves the best overall performance as a student
- ERes2Net (1.8M params) is the most parameter-efficient option
- **Cosine similarity** outperforms MSE, L1, KL-Divergence, and CLAP as the distillation loss

## Balanced Data Sampling Matters

One of our contributions is **Balanced Data Sampling (BDS)** — a cluster-aware sampling strategy. The impact is dramatic, especially for smaller architectures:

For ERes2Net, removing BDS causes a **26-point performance drop** — showing that cluster-aware sampling is critical for ultra-lightweight models.

## Try It Yourself

All pretrained models are available on [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/mohammedali2501/ssondo) and can be loaded in a few lines:

```python
from ssondo import get_ssondo, list_models

# List available models
for name, desc in list_models().items():
    print(f"{name}: {desc}")

# Load and use
model = get_ssondo("matpac-mobilenetv3")
embeddings = model(audio)
```

The full training pipeline is open-source on [GitHub](https://github.com/MedAliAdlouni/ssondo) under the MIT license.
